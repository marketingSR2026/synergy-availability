Injured at work in BC? Three steps to WCB physiotherapy

You can book before your claim number arrives.

  1. Right away

    Report the injury

    Tell your employer as soon as it is safe. Report to WorkSafeBC online any time, or call Teleclaim at 1.888.967.5377 (604.231.8888 in the Lower Mainland), weekdays 8 a.m. to 6 p.m.

  2. Next

    Book your assessment

    Pick the clinic nearest home or work and book a WCB Physio Initial Session. Tell us the injury happened at work.

  3. At your visit

    Bring your claim number

    WorkSafeBC mails an 8-digit claim number. Bring it if it has arrived. Contracted physiotherapists bill WorkSafeBC directly for the assessment.

Before you book

How WCB physio works at each claim stage

Who this is for

Workers in BC who were hurt at work and have reported the injury to WorkSafeBC, or are reporting it now. The claim can still be pending.

  1. Claim accepted. Care related to your claim is billed to WorkSafeBC and you should not have to pay fees.
  2. Claim pending. Your first assessment can go ahead without prior approval within 60 days of your injury. WorkSafeBC pays for that assessment even if the claim is later denied. Funded treatment visits start once the claim is accepted. Until then, a clinic may ask you to pay for treatment.
  3. More than 60 days since the injury. WorkSafeBC must approve treatment before it starts. It is not refused automatically.

For your first visit Checklist

  • Your 8-digit claim number, if it has arrived
  • Your employer’s name
  • The date and how the injury happened
  • Clothes you can move in

Common work injuries

Work injuries our physiotherapists treat

Your plan is built around what your job asks of your body, whether that is lifting on a site, driving, patient care or desk work.

  • Back strain from lifting

    Pain or stiffness after lifting, carrying, bending or twisting at work.

  • Shoulder strain and overuse

    Pain reaching overhead, pushing, pulling or carrying loads on one side.

  • Repetitive strain

    Wrist, elbow or forearm pain from repeated tasks, tools or keyboard work.

  • Neck strain

    Neck pain and stiffness after a strain, a long shift at a screen or a sudden movement.

  • Slips, trips and falls

    Sprains, strains and bruising after a fall on stairs, ladders, ice or wet floors.

  • Knee and ankle injuries

    Twists and sprains from climbing, kneeling, uneven ground or stepping down.

Your first visit

What happens at your WCB physio assessment

  • We ask how and when you were hurt at work, and what your job involves.
  • Your physiotherapist checks how you move, your strength and where it hurts.
  • You get a plan with goals tied to the tasks you need to get back to.
  • You leave with things you can do at home and at work while you recover.

Return to work

Back to work, one step at a time

  1. Phase 1

    Assess

    We look at your injury and the physical demands of your job.

  2. Phase 2

    Treat

    Hands-on care and exercise to settle pain and restore movement.

  3. Phase 3

    Build

    Graded exercise that works up to the lifting, standing or reaching your job needs.

  4. Phase 4

    Return

    Back to work, sometimes on modified duties first, with a plan for the rest.

If you need more treatment, your physiotherapist can ask WorkSafeBC for it. WorkSafeBC decides.

FAQ

WorkSafeBC physio questions

Is WCB the same as WorkSafeBC?

Yes. WorkSafeBC is the Workers’ Compensation Board of British Columbia, which is why many people still call it WCB. You may also see it written as WSBC.

Do I need a claim number before I book?

No. At a WorkSafeBC-contracted physiotherapist you can have your initial assessment before WorkSafeBC accepts your claim, if it is within 60 days of your injury. You do need to report the injury to WorkSafeBC so there is a claim, even if it is still pending. WorkSafeBC mails you an 8-digit claim number once it gets your report.

Do I need a doctor’s referral for WorkSafeBC physio?

WorkSafeBC tells workers who need physiotherapy to visit a contracted physiotherapist and explain that the injury is work-related. Its guidance for physiotherapy does not list a doctor’s referral as a step. If treatment starts more than 60 days after your injury, WorkSafeBC has to approve it first. Massage therapy is different: it needs a doctor’s referral and an accepted claim.

Will WCB pay for my physio, or will I have to pay?

Once your claim is accepted, yes. Your provider bills WorkSafeBC for care related to your claim, and you should not have to pay fees. While your claim is pending, a clinic may ask you to pay for treatment, and you can ask for a refund if the claim is accepted later. Ask the clinic how it handles visits while your claim is pending. Treatment for a body area that is not part of your accepted claim can be billed to you.

What happens if my claim is denied?

If your claim was pending at your first visit, WorkSafeBC still pays the contracted clinic for that initial assessment, even if the claim is later denied. WorkSafeBC does not pay for treatment visits given while the claim was pending if it is denied.

It has been more than 60 days since my injury. Can I still get physio?

Yes, but WorkSafeBC has to approve treatment first when it starts more than 60 days after the injury. It is not refused automatically.

How many physio visits does WorkSafeBC cover?

Treatment visits paid by WorkSafeBC start once your claim is accepted. If you need more treatment, your physiotherapist can ask WorkSafeBC for it, and WorkSafeBC decides. WorkSafeBC’s new physiotherapy services agreement took effect on April 1, 2026. If another page gives you a visit number, check it against WorkSafeBC’s own site, and ask your physiotherapist where your treatment stands on your claim.

What if I go to a physio who is not contracted with WorkSafeBC?

You pay for each visit yourself and ask WorkSafeBC to reimburse you at its set rate, which is $95 per visit from April 1, 2026. You pay any difference.

How do I report a workplace injury to WorkSafeBC?

Tell your employer as soon as it is safe, and tell anyone who treats you that it happened at work. Then report it to WorkSafeBC online at any time, or by phone through Teleclaim. Teleclaim is 1.888.967.5377 toll-free or 604.231.8888 in the Lower Mainland, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. You have up to one year to report, but report right away.
