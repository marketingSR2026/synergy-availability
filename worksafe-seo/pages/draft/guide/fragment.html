WorkSafeBC is the Workers’ Compensation Board of BC, so you will also see it called WCB. WorkSafeBC’s new physiotherapy services agreement took effect on April 1, 2026. This page is based on the WorkSafeBC pages listed at the bottom, checked on the dates shown there.

Claim number

Do I need a WorkSafeBC claim number before I book physio?

Short answer

You need to have reported the injury to WorkSafeBC, but your claim can still be pending. At a contracted physiotherapist, you can have your first assessment before WorkSafeBC accepts your claim if it is within 60 days of your injury. WorkSafeBC mails you an 8-digit claim number once it gets your report. Ask the clinic what to bring if your claim number has not arrived yet.

When you book, tell the clinic the injury happened at work. WorkSafeBC asks you to tell anyone who treats you that the injury happened at work.

TipWhen the letter with your claim number comes, take a photo of it. Then you will always have the number on your phone.

Reporting

How do I report a work injury to WorkSafeBC?

Short answer

First, tell your employer as soon as it is safe, and tell anyone who treats you that it happened at work. Then report it to WorkSafeBC. You can report online any time, day or night, or by phone through Teleclaim. You have up to one year from the injury to report it, but it is best to report right away.

  • Report online

    WorkSafeBC recommends reporting online for a physical injury from a single incident. Online reporting is open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

    WorkSafeBC lists what to have ready, such as your Personal Health Number and how the injury happened.

    Report an injury on worksafebc.com

  • Report by phone (Teleclaim)

    Call 1.888.967.5377 toll-free, or 604.231.8888 in the Lower Mainland. Lines are open Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. Pacific time.

    The phone may suit you better for an injury that came on slowly, a mental health injury or hearing loss.

    Call Teleclaim

What about Form 6 and Form 6A?

The paper Form 6 has been retired. Workers now report online or by phone. Form 6A is a notice you give to your employer, not to WorkSafeBC. Your employer may ask you to fill it in. We do not copy forms onto this page. Use WorkSafeBC’s own pages so you always get the current version.

  • Form 6 page on worksafebc.com
  • Form 6A page on worksafebc.com

Your employer has its own duty: it must report the injury to WorkSafeBC within 72 hours if you missed work or needed medical care beyond first aid.

Pending claims

Can I start physio while my claim is pending, and will I pay?

Short answer

Yes, you can start with an assessment. A contracted physiotherapist can do your first assessment on a pending claim, with no prior approval, if it is within 60 days of your injury. The clinic bills WorkSafeBC directly for that assessment. Treatment visits paid by WorkSafeBC can start only after the claim is accepted. Before then, a clinic may ask you to pay for treatment. If your claim is accepted later, you can ask the clinic for a refund. If the claim is denied, WorkSafeBC does not pay for treatment visits given while it was pending. Ask the clinic how it handles visits while your claim is pending.

Denied claims

What happens if my WorkSafeBC claim is denied?

Short answer

If your claim was pending at your first visit, WorkSafeBC still pays a contracted clinic for that first assessment, even if the claim is later denied. If the claim is denied, WorkSafeBC does not pay for treatment visits given while it was pending. Each clinic decides whether to ask you to pay for those visits or to wait for the decision before treating you. If you disagree with the decision, you can ask for a review within 90 days.

A denied claim is hard news, especially when you are still sore. Read the decision letter closely. The 90 days for a review count from the date of the decision. You can also contact the Workers’ Advisers Office for advice at any stage of your claim.

The 60-day rule

Can I still get physio more than 60 days after my injury?

Short answer

It is possible, but WorkSafeBC has to approve it first. It is not refused automatically, so 60 days is not a cut-off for physio. The 60 days is the window in which a contracted physio can do your first assessment without asking WorkSafeBC first. Tell the clinic your injury date when you book.

You may read online that WorkSafeBC physio only works in the first 60 days. WorkSafeBC’s rules do not end physio at 60 days. After 60 days, WorkSafeBC has to approve treatment before it starts. It is not refused automatically. If you have surgery related to your claim, the 60-day window for a first assessment without approval can count from the surgery date.

Fees

Will I pay anything once my claim is accepted?

Short answer

Once WorkSafeBC accepts your claim, your clinic bills WorkSafeBC for care related to your claim, and you should not have to pay any fees. A clinic with a WorkSafeBC contract is not allowed to charge you extra, such as a top-up or user fee, for services under that contract, except where the contract allows it. Costs can still come up, including in the cases below.

When costs can come up

  • While your claim is pending. A clinic may ask you to pay for treatment visits until the claim is accepted. How pending claims work
  • If your claim is denied. WorkSafeBC does not pay for treatment visits given while the claim was pending. Denied claims
  • Care for a body area that is not part of your claim. Treatment for a body area that is not part of your accepted claim is not covered by the claim and can be billed to you.
  • Services WorkSafeBC does not fund. At our clinics, if WorkSafeBC does not cover a service for your claim, or has not given approval for it, we can bill you for it.
  • A physio without a WorkSafeBC contract. WorkSafeBC repays you at its set rate, and you pay any difference. Non-contracted physios

If you are not sure what a visit will cost you, ask the clinic before your appointment.

Choosing a physio

Can I use a physio who is not contracted with WorkSafeBC?

Short answer

You can, but you pay the clinic yourself and WorkSafeBC repays only its set rate. WorkSafeBC asks workers who need physiotherapy to visit a physiotherapist who has a contract with WorkSafeBC in their region. If you choose one who is not contracted, you pay for each visit and then ask WorkSafeBC to pay you back. WorkSafeBC repays at its set rate, $95 per visit from April 1, 2026, and you pay any difference. If your claim is denied, WorkSafeBC does not pay for treatment given while it was pending, and care for a body area that is not part of your accepted claim can be billed to you.

That “you pay the difference” rule is for physios without a contract. A contracted clinic cannot charge you extra for services under its WorkSafeBC contract, except where the contract allows it.

How to check if a clinic is contracted

WorkSafeBC publishes regional lists of physiotherapy clinics contracted for musculoskeletal care, for example Metro Vancouver and the Fraser Valley. It keeps separate lists for other programs, so being on one list does not mean a clinic is on another. You can also simply ask the clinic when you call.

Extensions

How much physio does WorkSafeBC cover, and can I get more?

Short answer

If you need more treatment, your physiotherapist can ask WorkSafeBC for it, and WorkSafeBC decides whether to approve the request. WorkSafeBC’s new physiotherapy services agreement took effect on April 1, 2026. If you read about WorkSafeBC physio on an older page, check it against WorkSafeBC’s own site. Ask your physiotherapist where your treatment stands on your claim right now.

If your physiotherapist thinks you need more treatment, they can request it as part of your treatment plan. WorkSafeBC decides.

Other care on a claim

Does WorkSafeBC cover massage therapy or acupuncture?

Short answer

WorkSafeBC massage therapy has its own rules: it needs a doctor’s referral and an accepted claim. WorkSafeBC acupuncture needs an accepted claim but no referral. The treatment has to suit your accepted injury and focus on active rehab and getting back to work.

  • Massage therapy on a WCB claim

    You need a doctor’s referral and an accepted claim before treatment starts. More treatment after the first set needs WorkSafeBC approval.

    Massage therapy on worksafebc.com

  • Acupuncture on a WCB claim

    You need an accepted claim. No referral is needed. Past the first set of visits, a WorkSafeBC officer has to approve more.

    Acupuncture on worksafebc.com

FAQ

What else do injured workers ask about coverage?

Do I pay for physio on a WorkSafeBC claim?

On an accepted claim, care related to your claim is billed to WorkSafeBC, and you should not have to pay any fees. A contracted clinic also bills WorkSafeBC directly for your first assessment, even before the claim is accepted, if it is within 60 days of your injury. Treatment visits before acceptance are different: a clinic may ask you to pay, and you can ask for a refund if the claim is accepted later. If the claim is denied, WorkSafeBC does not pay for treatment visits given while it was pending. Care for a body area that is not part of your accepted claim can be billed to you. At a physio who is not contracted with WorkSafeBC, you pay and WorkSafeBC repays only its set rate.

How do WorkSafeBC claims work for physio?

Here is the usual order. Tell your employer. Report the injury to WorkSafeBC online or by phone. Book a first assessment with a contracted physio, even while the claim is pending, if it is within 60 days of your injury. Your 8-digit claim number comes by mail. Treatment visits paid by WorkSafeBC can start only after the claim is accepted. If you need more treatment later, your physio can ask WorkSafeBC for it. WorkSafeBC decides.

Do I need a doctor’s referral for WorkSafeBC physio?

WorkSafeBC’s advice is simple: if you need physiotherapy, go to a physiotherapist who has a contract with WorkSafeBC and explain that your injury is work-related. WorkSafeBC massage therapy has its own rules: it needs a doctor’s referral and an accepted claim.

What if I have not reported my injury yet?

Report it now. There needs to be a WorkSafeBC claim, even a pending one, for the clinic to bill WorkSafeBC. You can report online at any time, or call Teleclaim at 1.888.967.5377, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. Pacific time. You have up to one year from the injury, but sooner is better.

Do I have to tell my employer?

Yes. WorkSafeBC asks you to tell your employer about the injury as soon as it is safe to do so. Your employer must report it to WorkSafeBC within 72 hours if you missed work or needed medical care beyond first aid. Your employer may also ask you to fill in Form 6A, which goes to them, not to WorkSafeBC.

I had surgery for my work injury. Can I start physio after it?

Yes. A contracted physio can do a post-surgery assessment on your claim without asking WorkSafeBC first, as long as it is within 60 days of the surgery related to your claim. Bring any notes or exercise sheets your surgeon gave you to your first visit.
